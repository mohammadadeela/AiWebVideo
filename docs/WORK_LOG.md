## 2026-10-11 — Product links and map links: robustness, honesty, and a way to diagnose real links
Why these two kept failing in practice: every earlier check used pages and links I made up in a sandbox that cannot reach shops or Google, and there was never a real failing link to reproduce. What was actually wrong in the code:
- Maps: the route hard-coded `imageryAvailable: false`; satellite/street imagery is OFF unless `ARCHITECTURE_MAPS_IMAGERY=1` + `GOOGLE_MAPS_API_KEY` (an owner compliance decision), so by default the AI never SEES the real place; the short-link follower used a bot user-agent, refused any redirect that left google.com (Google's consent page = `consent.google.com`) and ignored the case where Google answers with a page instead of a redirect; coordinates typed by the customer were never read; addresses need `GOOGLE_MAPS_SERVER_API_KEY` to become a point.
- Products: bot-protection pages ("Robot Check", "Just a moment…", HTTP 200) were parsed as products and their logos offered as photos; WooCommerce's big-photo attribute (`data-large_image`) and common lazy-load attributes were not read.
Added: `lib/maps-resolve.ts` (typed decimal/DMS/geo: coordinates, OpenStreetMap + Apple links, short-link following with a browser user-agent, consent unwrapping, page-body extraction incl. meta refresh/canonical/static-map centre, step trace, loop-safe); the customer sees the exact coordinates, an embedded map of the point ("Is this the right spot?"), an honest imagery message and an "Open in Google Maps" link; `looksLikeBotWall` + step trace in the product reader; Admin > System > Link checker (`POST /api/admin/link-check`) that shows every step and the photos/point found, with "Copy report".
